tool-identity

Give a Vayu tool its identity and its screen's manners - a Sanskrit name, a logo, a real Sanskrit shloka as its tag, colour that means something, rounded corners, and forms that can't be changed by accident. Use whenever creating, renaming or publishing a Vayu tool, building or changing its screen, writing or changing its README, website, site.toml, logo or about screen, or checking a tool against these rules.

Use it: ask any agent in your own words. It picks this skill by the description above, opens these instructions and follows them. Already in every agent vishvakarma syncs.

Files

  • SKILL.md the instructions

#A tool's identity

Every Vayu tool has all three, with no exceptions:

  1. A Sanskrit name, from Hindu mythology, history or culture, chosen for what the tool does, with its Devanagari and its true meaning.
  2. A logo: assets/logo.svg, in the family's style (a rounded square tile with a gradient in the tool's colour and one simple line glyph). Copy another tool's file and change the colour, glyph and aria-label.
  3. A shloka: one real verse that suits the tool, shown as its tag.

#The shloka

  • Never write or adapt a verse. Quote a real one from a named work, exact to the syllable, and check it against a text you can read (for example gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in, sanskritdocuments.org, wisdomlib.org). Don't trust memory. If you can't verify it, pick another verse.
  • Choose it for the tool: ideally it holds the tool's own word or idea.
  • No two tools share a verse. Look at the others' site.toml first.
  • The English gloss may cover part of the verse but must not bend its meaning to fit the tool.

#Where it goes

  • site.toml: shloka (Devanagari, lines split with \n), shloka_meaning and shloka_source. The website's hero shows them (scripts/build-site.py).
  • README.md: at the very top, under the title, between <!-- identity --> and <!-- /identity -->: the logo, the verse in bold, then the gloss and source. Below it, the line saying what the name means.
  • The tool's own screen, if it has an about, settings or sidebar place for it: the logo and the verse. Always in Devanagari. Never as text in a terminal screen: tmux drops its vowel signs, which misquotes the verse. Draw it as a picture (vaikhari's internal/tui/picture.go does this in kitty), with Roman letters (IAST) only where pictures can't be drawn.

Then rebuild the site and add a CHANGELOG entry, as the tool's own notes say.

#Its screen

Colour that means something. A screen of one colour hides what matters. Use the theme's colours, never hard-coded ones, and always for the same meanings:

  • red: danger, errors, what can't be undone
  • green: done, working
  • yellow: waiting on you, not saved
  • peach: what to look for or copy, what an agent wants
  • blue and sky: networks and accounts, links
  • the accent: where you are now
  • muted: what's secondary

Leave ordinary text alone: when everything is coloured, nothing stands out.

Every corner is rounded. Frames, cards, bubbles, popups, buttons, progress bars and website boxes. In a terminal use ╭╮╰╯, even beside heavy lines (━┃): Unicode has no heavy arc, and ┌┐└┘ or ┏┓┗┛ are never used. In CSS, a visible corner never has border-radius: 0. Only what can't be rounded is exempt: fullscreen windows, and tmux's own pane borders.

Nothing changes by accident. A settings or credentials form opens read-only. Only Enter starts editing a field, never a letter key, because stray typing contains letters. Esc cancels that field. Saving is its own key, and a change that signs someone out or can't be undone asks first.
